# Duckwalk

Harold, a rubber debugging duck, keeps your computer awake and works as its remote.

When you step away, Harold moves the mouse pointer so the lock timer, the screen saver and your "away" status still see someone at the desk. Then he puts it back where it was. It works over **USB** or **Bluetooth**, with no drivers and nothing to install on the computer.

## Keep-awake modes

- **Walk:** a short, curved, random walk every few minutes, never the same path twice
- **Subtle:** one pixel out and back, invisible while you work
- **Wander:** the pointer drifts around without stopping and glides back to where it started when you turn it off

## Buttons

- **OK:** wake Harold or put him to sleep; hold to quack
- **Right:** Settings
- **Left:** Remote for media, slides or the mouse
- **Up:** Preview the next walk
- **Down:** Connection check
- **Back:** leave; USB and Bluetooth go back to normal

## Settings

- How often and how far Harold walks
- Work hours only (Monday to Friday, 8 to 18)
- **Stop after** 1 to 8 hours with nobody around, so a computer you walked away from still locks
- Sound and vibration for the quack

## The desktop app

The optional desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux tells Harold when you use your own mouse or keyboard, so he lets go of the pointer while you work. It lives in the tray. Get it from the [Duckwalk releases](https://github.com/arudaev/duckwalk/releases).

## Before you use it at work

A keep-awake tool can be against your employer's rules. Duckwalk only moves the pointer; it never types.
